## One day in Auserene: how the pieces connect

A feature list can't show this part, so here's an ordinary Tuesday.

**2:14 p.m.** A call with your sister leaves you rattled. From the Home Screen widget you type one line: "call with my sister, same fight again." You drag the mood slider down, pick "resentful" on the Feelings Wheel and tag family as the cause. Ten seconds.

**6:40 p.m.** On the train home you start Breathe on your Apple Watch. Two minutes of haptic breathing with your phone in your pocket and your wrist down.

**9:30 p.m.** The evening conversation opens on the call, because that's what you wrote and where your mood dropped. Auserene already knows who "my sister" is and that something like this came up two weeks ago, so it asks whether it's the same thing. Ten minutes of talking later, the conversation is your journal entry. You never wrote a paragraph.

**9:45 p.m.** You make tonight's meditation. Fifteen minutes, rain underneath, your name said the way you say it, and the part about putting something down is about that call, not a script recorded for millions of people.

**The next morning.** The You tab has a new line under what gets to you, something like "calls that reopen old arguments with your sister." Keep it, edit it or delete it. Your sister's page holds the moment, and over the weeks it builds insights into how things are between you. If you added her birthday, the reminder is already set.

**Sunday.** Week in review looks back on all seven days. If "a walk after hard calls" keeps turning up under what seems to help, you turn that insight into a habit goal: who you want to be, the practice, a time, a finish line of 21 days. You check in with a tap on your phone or your watch. A missed day never turns red.

That's the difference. Rosebud has a diary, a mood tracker, a goal tracker and a weekly report, and it does each one well. In Auserene each piece hands off to the next, so the app gets more useful the more you live in it.

## What you get with Auserene

Auserene is an AI journal for iPhone and iPad, with a companion app for Apple Watch. Here's what's inside, and what each part hands to the others.

- **Notes your way.** Type a line, snap a photo, record a voice note up to 10 minutes in any of over 100 languages, or scan a handwritten page into text on your phone. Stuck? "Help me write" offers three ways to start. Everything you leave is what the evening conversation reads.
- **The evening conversation.** Auserene reads your day and asks about the parts that seem to matter. The conversation becomes your entry.
- **A meditation from your day.** 5, 15 or 25 minutes, written from tonight's conversation, read with your name, with 11 ambient sounds to mix underneath. It plays offline once you've heard it.
- **Chat any time.** A conversation that already knows your people and your context. Share a photo, have replies read aloud, or go incognito. When you don't know where to start, 13 guided conversations (untangle a thought, settle your body, loosen a worry, think it through) and personal conversation ideas written from what you've talked about lately.
- **Memory you can read.** The You tab lists what Auserene has learned: what seems to help, what matters to you, what gets to you, threads still open. Edit or delete any line.
- **People.** A page for everyone who matters, filled with the moments you share and insights built from what you've said about them, plus birthday reminders.
- **Habit goals.** Start from who you want to be, or from an insight in the You tab. No streaks to break.
- **Moods and patterns.** A Feelings Wheel of more than 120 words and what caused each feeling, moods month by month, a week in review, and a heatmap of your last three months.
- **Lines worth keeping.** Press and hold any line to keep it, and share it as a quote card.
- **On your wrist and Home Screen.** The Apple Watch app, widgets for a quick note or the day's quote, Face ID lock, gentle reminders, and a look that follows the time of day.
- **Eight guided journeys** for sleep, an anxious mind, focus, pressure, self-kindness and noticing the good, a few minutes a day.

## What is Rosebud?

Rosebud is an AI journal app from Just Imagine, Inc. Its [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/rosebud-ai-journal-diary/id6451135127), titled "Rosebud: AI Journal & Diary," lists an interactive daily diary, expert-crafted experiences, voice journaling, pattern recognition, a smart mood tracker, a smart goal tracker, daily quotes and weekly growth insights. It had a 4.9 rating from 3.4K ratings when we checked.

Its [homepage](https://www.rosebud.app/) sells it as "the world's best AI journal." The [evening reflection](https://help.rosebud.app/daily-journaling/evening-reflection) asks for a highlight, a source of tension and something you learned, based on the Rose, Bud, Thorn exercise. [Guided journals](https://help.rosebud.app/daily-journaling/guided-journals) include Reframing Negative Thoughts (CBT) and Acceptance and Commitment (ACT), and the homepage shows an Internal Family Systems journal. You can pick one of five [AI personas](https://help.rosebud.app/ai-analysis/ai-personalization), such as The Sage or The Challenger. The [weekly report](https://help.rosebud.app/ai-analysis/weekly-report) shows writing stats, mood trends, key themes, a "cast of characters" and up to five insights.

Rosebud's [limitations page](https://help.rosebud.app/getting-started/rosebud's-limitations) says it is "not a replacement for professional mental health care." Neither is Auserene.

## Which one remembers you better?

Auserene, because you can see what it remembers.

Rosebud's [long-term memory](https://help.rosebud.app/ai-analysis/long-term-memory) is "automatically enabled for Rosebud Bloom subscribers." It notes the important things you mention and how people and events relate. Rosebud is candid about a limit: it "doesn't have a great sense of time, and does better recalling topics vs. specific dates." What it doesn't state is whether you can see, edit or delete individual memories. The settings you can manage are [learned preferences](https://help.rosebud.app/ai-analysis/learned-preferences), which cover how replies are written.

Auserene's memory is built to be read. Every line it has learned sits in the You tab in plain sentences, and you can edit or delete any of them. It keeps track of who's who, so "my sister" always means the same person, and that memory reaches everything else: the evening conversation, the chat, your people's pages, the meditation.

A journal that remembers you is making claims about you. You deserve to read them and correct them.

## Privacy: Rosebud vs Auserene

Auserene's [privacy policy](/privacy-policy) says your journal is encrypted with AES-256 at rest, using a key unique to you, and travels over TLS. Our team doesn't read your journal, unless you choose to share a conversation with us in a feedback report. The AI that reads your journal works under zero-data-retention terms, keeps nothing after a reply and is contractually barred from training on your words. No ads, no ad trackers, photos of people stay on your phone, and you can delete everything from inside the app.

Rosebud's [privacy policy](https://help.rosebud.app/about-us/privacy-policy) says data is encrypted in transit and at rest, names OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Amazon Web Services and Groq as AI providers under BAAs and zero-data-retention agreements, and limits staff access to a small number of people for support, technical issues and safety. Its [ChatGPT comparison page](https://help.rosebud.app/comparison/chatgpt-vs.-rosebud) says your input isn't used to train AI models.

Neither is end-to-end encrypted. Any journal that sends your words to an AI has to process them at that moment.
