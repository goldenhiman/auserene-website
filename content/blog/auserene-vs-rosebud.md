Auserene and Rosebud are both AI journal apps that talk back, remember what you've written and look for patterns over time. The difference is what they do with it. Rosebud is built around guided journaling: daily prompts, therapy-informed guided journals (CBT, ACT, IFS), AI personas and a weekly insight report, on iPhone, Android and the web. Auserene is built around one evening conversation that reads the notes you left during the day, writes the journal entry for you, and then turns that day into a guided meditation made from your own words. It is iPhone only, and what it remembers about you sits on a page you can read and edit.

We make Auserene, so read this with that in mind. Every fact about Rosebud below comes from Rosebud's own website, help center, privacy policy or App Store listing, checked on November 19, 2026, and each one links to its source. Where Rosebud doesn't say something, we say "not stated" rather than guess. (Searching for "Rosebud AI" also brings up rosebud.ai, an unrelated game-making tool. This page is about the Rosebud journal at rosebud.app.)

## What is Rosebud?

Rosebud is an AI journal app from Just Imagine, Inc. Its [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/rosebud-ai-journal-diary/id6451135127), titled "Rosebud: AI Journal & Diary," lists an interactive daily diary, expert-crafted experiences, voice journaling, intelligent pattern recognition, a smart mood tracker, a smart goal tracker, daily quotes and weekly personal growth insights. It had a 4.9 rating from 3.4K ratings when we checked.

Rosebud's [homepage](https://www.rosebud.app/) sells it as "the world's best AI journal" and promises you'll "feel better in minutes, not months." Its sections are "Connect the dots" (memory), "Uncover your patterns," "Reach your goals," "Speak freely" (voice) and "Build resilience."

The help center fills in the details. The [evening reflection](https://help.rosebud.app/daily-journaling/evening-reflection) runs between noon and 3 a.m. and asks for a highlight, a source of tension and something you learned, based on the Rose, Bud, Thorn exercise. [Guided journals](https://help.rosebud.app/daily-journaling/guided-journals) include Reframing Negative Thoughts (CBT), a gratitude journal and Acceptance and Commitment (ACT), and the homepage shows a guided Internal Family Systems journal. You can pick one of five [AI personas](https://help.rosebud.app/ai-analysis/ai-personalization), such as The Sage or The Challenger, each with its own instructions, model and voice. The [weekly report](https://help.rosebud.app/ai-analysis/weekly-report) shows writing stats, an "emotional landscape" of mood trends, key themes, a "cast of characters" and up to five insights, with a deeper analysis once you write more than 1,500 words in a week.

Rosebud's [therapy and coaching page](https://www.rosebud.app/therapy-and-coaching) says it was "co-designed with coaches and therapists" to "bridge the gap between sessions," and its [limitations page](https://help.rosebud.app/getting-started/rosebud's-limitations) says plainly that it is "not a replacement for professional mental health care" and can't handle a crisis.

## What is Auserene?

Auserene is an AI journal for iPhone that remembers what helps you. During the day you leave quick notes: a line of text, a photo, a voice note, a scanned page from a paper journal, or a mood on a slider and a Feelings Wheel. In the evening, Auserene reads those notes and holds a short conversation about how the day went, asking about the parts that seem to matter. The conversation becomes the day's journal entry. Before bed, it can write a guided meditation from that conversation, five, 15 or 25 minutes long, read aloud with your name and the specifics of your day.

Around that loop sit a chat for any time of day, 13 guided conversations for when you don't know where to start, eight guided meditation journeys, habit goals, a page for each person in your life, and a You tab that shows what Auserene has learned about you. Our [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/auserene-ai-journal-habits/id6783458046) has the full list.

## How do they journal differently?

Rosebud gives you more ways in. You can do a morning intention, an evening reflection, a guided CBT or ACT journal, a custom journal you design, or a free-form entry where it asks follow-up questions to help you dig deeper. If you like structure and variety, and you want a journal that teaches you therapy-style exercises, that range is Rosebud's strength.

Auserene gives you one main way in and tries to make it effortless. You don't sit down to write a full entry. You leave fragments during the day, and the evening conversation turns them into the entry. The 13 guided conversations (untangle a thought, settle your body, loosen a worry, a kinder voice, three good things, think a decision through, put something down) are there for the moments you want a specific exercise, but most days the evening chat is the whole practice.

Which suits you depends on how you journal now. People who already sit down to write tend to like Rosebud's prompts. People who keep meaning to journal and don't often find it easier to leave a line at 2 p.m. and talk for five minutes at night.

## Which one remembers you better?

Both have long-term memory, and both put it behind the paid plan.

Rosebud's [long-term memory](https://help.rosebud.app/ai-analysis/long-term-memory) is "automatically enabled for Rosebud Bloom subscribers." As you write, it notes the important things you mention and how people and events relate, and recalls them later. Rosebud is candid about one limit: it "doesn't have a great sense of time, and does better recalling topics vs. specific dates." An opt-in memory precision setting helps with time questions at the cost of slower replies. What Rosebud doesn't state is whether you can see, edit or delete individual memories. The settings it does let you manage are [learned preferences](https://help.rosebud.app/ai-analysis/learned-preferences), which cover the style and format of replies.

Auserene's memory is built to be read. Everything it has learned sits in the You tab in plain sentences: what seems to help you, what matters to you, what gets to you, and threads that are still open. If something is wrong or out of date, you edit or delete it and Auserene adjusts. It also keeps track of who's who, so "my sister" always means the same person.

That difference matters more than it sounds. A journal that remembers you is making claims about you. You should be able to read those claims and correct them.

## Mood tracking, patterns and insights

Rosebud's pattern tools are its [weekly report](https://help.rosebud.app/ai-analysis/weekly-report), the smart mood tracker and auto-tagging. The weekly report is the centerpiece: mood trends, recurring themes, the people who came up, and a handful of insights, with more depth when you write more.

Auserene tracks moods from the slider and Feelings Wheel, which also records what caused each feeling, and shows them month by month. The You tab lists the patterns it has found, a week in review looks back on the last seven days, and each person's page has insights into how things are with them, built from what you've said about that person. A heatmap shows your last three months of notes, chats, journal entries and meditations. You can turn any insight into a habit goal.

If you want one rich report a week, Rosebud's is more developed. If you want patterns that live alongside the people and goals they're about, Auserene spreads them through the app.

## Does Rosebud have meditation?

Not that it states. We found no meditation or breathing feature on Rosebud's homepage, App Store listing or help center, and its roadmap isn't public.

Meditation is half of Auserene. After the evening conversation, it writes a guided meditation from what you talked about, so the part about letting go is about the thing you actually need to let go of. You choose five, 15 or 25 minutes and an ambient sound (11 of them, from rain to a Tibetan bowl), and audio plays offline once you've heard it. There are also eight guided journeys, a few minutes a day, for sleep, an anxious mind, focus, pressure, self-kindness and noticing the good.

If you've been using Rosebud for journaling and a separate app for meditation, this is the main reason to try Auserene. Our post on [personalized meditation](/blog/personalized-meditation) covers what the research says about meditations made from your own day.

## Voice journaling

Rosebud's [voice journaling](https://help.rosebud.app/tools-for-growth/voice-journaling) has a hands-free mode, an auto-stop timer and a glossary of up to 10 words it should spell right, and it can read replies aloud. Its help center says its older voices retire on December 31, 2026, with new voices from ElevenLabs. One App Store reviewer reported lost voice recordings, and the developer replied that it was working on voice reliability; that's one user's report, not a measured problem.

Auserene's voice notes run up to 10 minutes and are transcribed in over 100 languages, and you can have chat replies read aloud. Voice notes are part of Premium.

## Does Rosebud still have a free plan?

No. Rosebud's help center says: "Effective September 30, 2026, the Rosebud free plan will be retired," because it is "unable to sustainably offer a free tier moving forward" with its new memory system ([source](https://help.rosebud.app/account/changes-to-the-free-plan)). Free users were offered a special deal inside the app, and Rosebud says nothing you've written goes away and you can [export everything](https://help.rosebud.app/account/export-data) as Markdown.

The [homepage](https://www.rosebud.app/) still said "Rosebud is free to use" when we checked on November 19, 2026, so the two pages disagree. The help center is the more specific of the two.

Auserene has a free plan with no time limit: notes with photos, page scanning, mood logging, an evening walk through your notes on your own, three "Help me write" nudges a day, all free guided journeys and ambient sounds, habit goals, people you add, reminders and widgets. The conversations, memory, voice notes and the meditation from your day are Premium.

## Privacy: Rosebud vs Auserene

Rosebud's [privacy policy](https://help.rosebud.app/about-us/privacy-policy) (last updated September 8, 2026) says data is encrypted in transit and at rest. It names OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Amazon Web Services and Groq as AI providers, says it has business associate agreements (BAAs) and zero-data-retention agreements with them, and calls its handling "HIPAA-aligned." Staff access to journal content is limited to a small number of people for support, technical issues and safety. Its [ChatGPT comparison page](https://help.rosebud.app/comparison/chatgpt-vs.-rosebud) says your input isn't used to train AI models. Account deletion removes your journal data, with backups kept up to 30 days.

Auserene's [privacy policy](/privacy-policy) says your writing is encrypted with a key tied to your account and that no one on our team reads it. Our AI providers work under zero-data-retention contracts, keep nothing after a reply, and are contractually barred from training on your words. There are no ads or ad trackers, photos of people stay on your phone, and you can delete your account and everything in it from inside the app.

Neither app is end-to-end encrypted. Any journal that sends your words to an AI model has to process them unencrypted at that moment. The fair comparison is who the providers are, what the contracts say, and whether staff can read your entries.

## Which should you choose?

Choose Rosebud if:

1. You want therapy-informed guided journals, such as CBT, ACT or IFS exercises.
2. You journal on Android, a Mac or a laptop browser.
3. You want a detailed weekly report and AI personas with different styles.
4. You're happy to pay for the app from day one.

Choose Auserene if:

1. You want a meditation made from your own day, not a separate meditation app.
2. You'd rather leave quick notes and talk for five minutes than sit down to write.
3. You want to read and edit everything the AI remembers about you.
4. You want people, habit goals and moods tied to the same memory.
5. You want a free plan you can keep using.

Neither is therapy. If your nights are mostly worry or low mood, please talk to a doctor or a mental health professional, and if you're in danger now, our [crisis resources page](/crisis-resources) lists free helplines by country.

## Questions people ask about Auserene and Rosebud

### Is Auserene better than Rosebud?

It depends on what you want. Rosebud is stronger on guided, therapy-informed journals, weekly reports and platforms (it runs on Android and the web). Auserene is stronger if you want a meditation made from your day, a memory you can read and edit, and a free plan. Auserene is iPhone only.

### Is Rosebud free?

Not anymore. Rosebud retired its free plan on September 30, 2026, according to its help center, though its homepage still says it's free to use. Rosebud Bloom costs $12.99 a month or $107.99 a year in the US.

### Does Rosebud remember past entries?

Yes, for Bloom subscribers. Rosebud's long-term memory recalls people, topics and how they relate, and Rosebud says it does better with topics than with specific dates. It doesn't state whether you can view or edit individual memories.

### Does Rosebud have guided meditation?

Rosebud doesn't list a meditation or breathing feature on its site, App Store listing or help center. Auserene writes a guided meditation from each day's conversation and has eight guided meditation journeys.

### What is a good Rosebud alternative for iPhone?

Auserene, if you want an evening conversation, editable memory and a meditation from your day. Day One with its Gold plan, if you want a long-term archive with AI added. Apple Journal, if you want something free and end-to-end encrypted with no AI chat. Our [Rosebud alternatives guide](/blog/rosebud-alternatives) compares nine.
