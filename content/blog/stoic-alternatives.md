## How to choose

1. **Decide whether you want routines or a conversation.** Stoic and the Five Minute Journal give you questions to fill in each morning and night. Auserene, Rosebud, Reflection and Day One's Daily Chat talk with you about what happened.
2. **Decide what meditation means to you.** A library of sessions and breathing techniques (Stoic), one made from your day (Auserene), or a separate meditation app.
3. **Check whether you can read the memory.** An app that remembers you should show you what it remembers. Auserene lists it and lets you edit it, and Mindsera's [Memory Profile](https://mindsera.com/help/account/memory-profile) lets you view, edit or delete it; Day One doesn't in its Apple apps; Stoic and Rosebud don't state it.
4. **Think about streaks honestly.** If they keep you going, Stoic, Daylio, the Five Minute Journal, Mindsera and Apple Journal all have them. If they've made you quit before, pick an app without them or turn them off.
5. **Know where your words go.** Stoic keeps entries on your device and iCloud until you use AI. Apple Journal and Day One offer end-to-end encryption. Any app that sends your words to a cloud model has to read them to reply, so look at who the provider is and whether it trains on your data.
6. **Match the platform to your life.** If you write on a laptop or an Android phone, an iPhone-only app won't fit.
7. **Try a week of evenings.** Notice whether you close the app calmer or more wound up. That tells you more than any table, this one included.

None of these apps is therapy, ours included, and Stoic says the same about itself. If you're struggling, please talk to a doctor or a mental health professional, and if you're in danger now, our [crisis resources page](/crisis-resources) lists free helplines by country.
