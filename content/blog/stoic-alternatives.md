## How to choose

1. **Pick an app where the pieces connect.** A morning note that shapes the evening conversation, a meditation made from it, and insights that become habits do more than five separate tools. Auserene is built this way. Most apps here keep those pieces apart.
2. **Routines or a conversation?** Stoic and the Five Minute Journal give you questions to fill in. Auserene, Rosebud, Reflection and Day One's Daily Chat talk with you.
3. **Decide what meditation means to you.** A library (Stoic), one made from your day (Auserene), or a separate app.
4. **Check whether you can read the memory.** Auserene lists it and lets you edit it, and Mindsera's [Memory Profile](https://mindsera.com/help/account/memory-profile) lets you view, edit or delete it. Day One doesn't in its Apple apps, and Stoic and Rosebud don't state it.
5. **Think about streaks honestly.** Stoic, Daylio, the Five Minute Journal, Mindsera and Apple Journal have them. If they've made you quit before, pick an app without them.
6. **Know where your words go.** Stoic keeps entries on your device and iCloud until you use AI. Apple Journal and Day One offer end-to-end encryption. With cloud AI, check who the provider is and whether it trains on your words.
7. **Match the platform to your life.** If you write on a laptop or an Android phone, an app made for iPhone and iPad won't fit. If your life runs on an iPhone and an Apple Watch, it will.

None of these apps is therapy, ours included, and Stoic says the same about itself.
