Auserene vs Day One comes down to what you want a journal to be. Day One is a journal for life: an archive of entries, photos, video and audio on almost every device, with end-to-end encryption, print books, and, since March 2026, an AI layer called Daily Chat in its $74.99-a-year Gold plan. Auserene is an AI journal for iPhone built around one evening conversation that reads the notes you left during the day, writes the entry with you, and then turns that day into a guided meditation. Day One wins on platforms, archive, encryption and price. Auserene wins if you want the AI to be the center of the practice, to show you everything it remembers, and to help you wind down afterward.

We make Auserene, so read this with that in mind. Every fact about Day One below comes from Day One's own website, help guides, blog or App Store listing, checked on November 19, 2026, and each one links to its source. Where Day One doesn't say something, we say "not stated" rather than guess.

## What is Day One?

Day One calls itself "your journal for life" and "the #1 journaling app" on its [homepage](https://dayoneapp.com/). It's owned by Automattic, and its [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/day-one-daily-journal-diary/id1044867788) had a 4.8 rating from 119K ratings when we checked. The listing describes it as Apple's App of the Year with over 15 million downloads.

Its strengths are the ones it has built for a decade. You get multiple journals, rich text and markdown, photos, video, audio with transcription, drawings, PDF scanning, templates, daily prompts, streaks, On This Day, a map view, and integrations with Strava, Zapier and IFTTT ([pricing](https://dayoneapp.com/pricing/)). You can print your journal as a book. It runs on iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Watch, Android, Windows and in any browser.

In March 2026, Day One [renamed Premium to Silver and added Gold](https://dayoneapp.com/blog/introducing-day-one-silver-and-gold/), a tier for "tools designed to help you reflect more deeply." Gold is where all the AI lives.

## What is Auserene?

Auserene is an AI journal for iPhone that remembers what helps you. During the day you leave quick notes: a line of text, a photo, a voice note, a scanned page from a paper journal, or a mood on a slider and a Feelings Wheel. In the evening, Auserene reads those notes and holds a short conversation about how the day went, asking about the parts that seem to matter. The conversation becomes the day's journal entry. Before bed, it can write a guided meditation from that conversation, read aloud with your name said the way you say it.

Around that loop sit a chat for any time of day, 13 guided conversations, habit goals, a page for each person in your life, and a You tab that shows what Auserene has learned about you. Our [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/auserene-ai-journal-habits/id6783458046) has the full list.

## Day One Daily Chat vs Auserene's evening conversation

These are the two features most worth comparing, because they do something similar.

Day One's [Daily Chat page](https://dayoneapp.com/features/daily-chat/) pitches it as a new way to journal "without the blank page." According to its [guide](https://dayoneapp.com/guides/ai-features/daily-chat/), it lives in a Today tab. It opens with a prompt, asks follow-up questions about your day, and when you're done you tap Generate Entry to turn the conversation into a journal entry you can edit. The chat stays open all day, so you can come back and update the entry, and you can go back to past days and chat about them. It works in any language, and a Voice Mode, which Day One says runs on OpenAI's Realtime API, lets you talk out loud. There's a daily usage limit that resets 24 hours after your first message, and Day One doesn't publish the number.

Auserene's evening conversation starts from somewhere different. It reads the notes, photos, voice notes and moods you left during the day and asks first about what seems to matter, so if you logged a low mood at lunch and mentioned the same meeting twice, that's where it goes. Because it remembers earlier weeks, it can ask whether something from last Tuesday came up again. The conversation becomes the entry, and then the day can become a meditation.

The practical difference is effort. Daily Chat is a nicer way to write a Day One entry. Auserene is built so that the notes you drop in at 2 p.m. do half the work by 9 p.m.

## Day One AI memory: which one remembers you better?

Both build a memory. Only one lets you read it on your iPhone.

Day One says Daily Chat "builds a memory of things you've shared," including your routines, relationships and recurring themes, and uses it to make conversations more personal. Those memories are stored encrypted in your account. Its guide is direct about one limit: "Reviewing or deleting individual memories isn't available in the iPhone, iPad, or Mac apps." It doesn't say whether Android or the web allow it. The control it describes is turning AI features off entirely in Settings.

Auserene's memory is built to be read. Everything it has learned sits in the You tab in plain sentences: what seems to help you, what matters to you, what gets to you, and threads still open. If something is wrong or out of date, you edit or delete it and the next conversation reflects that. It also keeps track of who's who, so "my sister" always means the same person.

## Mood, patterns and meditation

Day One's other AI features are about the writing itself. According to its [AI features guide](https://dayoneapp.com/guides/ai-features/ai-features/), Go Deeper suggests prompts based on what you've written, Entry Highlights pulls out the key themes and feelings of an entry, Title Suggestions does what it says, AI Image Generation illustrates an entry, Multi Entry Summary summarizes entries from the same date across years, and AI Day Summary (iPhone and iPad) summarizes a day from its entries, location visits and calendar events. We found no mood tracker, meditation or breathing feature on Day One's pricing page, features page or App Store listing; the listing does say it saves mindfulness minutes to the Health app.

Auserene tracks moods from a slider and an 82-word Feelings Wheel that also records what caused each feeling, and shows them month by month. The You tab lists patterns it has found, a week in review looks back on the last seven days, and each person's page has insights built from what you've said about them. You can turn any insight into a habit goal.

Then there's meditation, which is half of Auserene. After the evening conversation it writes a guided meditation from what you talked about, five, 15 or 25 minutes long, with one of 11 ambient sounds under the voice. There are eight guided journeys too, and audio plays offline once heard. Our post on [personalized meditation](/blog/personalized-meditation) covers why a meditation made from your own day is different from a library recording.

## Where Day One is clearly better

It's worth being plain about this.

1. **Platforms.** Day One runs on iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Watch, Android, Windows and the web. Auserene is iPhone only.
2. **The archive.** Unlimited photos and video (30 per entry), audio, drawings, PDFs, map and calendar views, On This Day, search and filters. Auserene is built around today, not a decade of media.
3. **Print books.** Day One prints your journal, with 35% off on Gold. Auserene doesn't.
4. **End-to-end encryption.** Day One offers it on every plan, including free. Auserene isn't end-to-end encrypted.
5. **Export.** Day One's listing lists export to PDF, plain text and more. Auserene doesn't list export.
6. **Price for AI.** Gold is $74.99 a year. Auserene Premium is $105.99 a year.
7. **Integrations.** Strava, Zapier, IFTTT, email-to-journal and Siri Shortcuts.

If those are what you value, Day One is the better app, and Daily Chat may be all the AI you need.

## How much does Day One cost? Is Day One Gold worth it?

Day One's [pricing page](https://dayoneapp.com/pricing/) lists three plans as of November 19, 2026.

1. **Basic**, free: unlimited entries and journals, one photo per entry, end-to-end encryption, prompts, templates, search, On This Day and streaks, on one device.
2. **Silver**, $49.99 a year: everything in Basic plus unlimited photos and video, sync across all your devices, audio with transcription, integrations, PDF scanning, drawings and Day One Labs. The App Store also lists Silver at $8.99 a month.
3. **Gold**, $74.99 a year: everything in Silver plus Daily Chat, reflective AI summaries and prompts, Go Deeper, entry summaries, title suggestions, image generation and 35% off printed journals. A monthly Gold price isn't stated on the pricing page or App Store listing.

Day One offers a one-month free trial inside the app, and notes that the App Store may not give you a second trial if you already tried Silver. Its AI features have daily usage limits.

Is Day One Gold worth it? If you already pay for Silver and want Daily Chat, Gold is $25 a year more and keeps everything you have. That's good value for an AI layer on a journal you already use. It's less good value if what you want is memory you can review on your iPhone, mood tracking or meditation, because Gold doesn't list them.

Auserene Premium costs $11.99 a month or $105.99 a year, about $8.83 a month, and both plans start with seven days free. There is one paid tier and no weekly plan.

Prices vary by country and change often. These are US prices from each company's own pages.

## Privacy: Day One end-to-end encryption and AI

Day One's [end-to-end encryption FAQ](https://dayoneapp.com/guides/day-one-sync/end-to-end-encryption-faq/) says entries are encrypted with a private key on your device before they reach its servers, and that Day One "never has access to it." That's real protection, and it's on the free plan.

AI changes the picture, and Day One says so. Its [AI features guide](https://dayoneapp.com/guides/ai-features/ai-features/) explains that the content a feature needs is decrypted on your device and sent over HTTPS to Day One's servers and then to an AI service, and that "content from end-to-end encrypted journals is temporarily not end-to-end encrypted while being processed with AI." Daily Chat goes to a third-party provider, "currently OpenAI"; some features run on infrastructure hosted by Automattic; and on supported Apple devices, Go Deeper, Title Suggestions and Entry Highlights can run on the device with Apple Intelligence. Day One doesn't publish which model it uses. It says requests aren't kept on its servers after processing and your entries aren't used to train AI models without explicit permission. AI needs your consent before first use, and you can turn it off.

Auserene's [privacy policy](/privacy-policy) says your writing is encrypted with a key tied to your account and that no one on our team reads it. Our AI providers work under zero-data-retention contracts, keep nothing after a reply, and are contractually barred from training on your words. There are no ads or ad trackers, and you can delete your account and everything in it from inside the app. Auserene is not end-to-end encrypted, because the AI has to read your words to reply.

When you use AI, neither app keeps your words from the AI provider while it replies. The difference is that Day One end-to-end encrypts everything you write without AI, and Auserene doesn't.

## Which should you choose?

Choose Day One if:

1. You want a journal for decades, with photos, video and print books.
2. You write on Android, Windows, a Mac or the web.
3. End-to-end encryption matters more to you than AI.
4. You want AI as an occasional extra, at $74.99 a year.

Choose Auserene if:

1. You want the AI conversation to be the practice, starting from notes you left during the day.
2. You want to read, edit and delete everything the AI remembers about you on your phone.
3. You want a meditation made from your own day, not a separate meditation app.
4. You want moods, people and habit goals tied to the same memory.

Some people will use both, Day One for the archive and Auserene for the evening. Neither is therapy. ## Questions people ask about Auserene and Day One

### Is Auserene better than Day One?

It depends on what you want. Day One is better for a long-term archive with photos and video, for Android, Windows and the web, for print books and for end-to-end encryption, and its AI plan costs less. Auserene is better if you want an evening AI conversation built from your day's notes, a memory you can read and edit, and a meditation made from your day. Auserene is iPhone only.

### Does Day One have AI?

Yes, on the Gold plan. Day One's AI features include Daily Chat, Go Deeper prompts, entry highlights, title suggestions, image generation and summaries. Daily Chat uses OpenAI, and some features can run on-device with Apple Intelligence.

### What is Day One Daily Chat?

Daily Chat is Day One's AI conversation feature. It asks questions about your day and turns the conversation into a journal entry you can edit. It builds memories of your routines and relationships, but Day One says you can't review or delete individual memories in its iPhone, iPad or Mac apps.

### How much is Day One Gold?

Day One Gold costs $74.99 a year in the US as of November 2026. Silver costs $49.99 a year, and Basic is free. Day One offers a one-month free trial in the app. A monthly Gold price isn't listed on its pricing page.

### Is Day One Gold worth it?

If you already use Day One and want Daily Chat and AI summaries, Gold costs $25 a year more than Silver and keeps everything Silver has. If you want memory you can review on iPhone, mood tracking or meditation, Gold doesn't list them.

### Is Day One still end-to-end encrypted if I use AI?

Day One offers end-to-end encryption on every plan. Its AI guide says content from end-to-end encrypted journals is temporarily not end-to-end encrypted while being processed by AI, and that content isn't used to train AI models. Auserene isn't end-to-end encrypted at all.
