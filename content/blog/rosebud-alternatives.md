The best Rosebud alternative depends on why you're leaving. If it's the price now that Rosebud's free plan is gone, Reflection, Life Note and Mindsera still have free tiers with some AI, and Apple Journal is free with no AI chat. If you want long-term memory you can actually read and edit, or a meditation made from your own day, look at Auserene. If you want a long archive across every device with AI as an add-on, Day One. If you want Rosebud's style on Android and the web, Reflection, Life Note and Mindsera all run there too.

## Why people look for a Rosebud alternative

Rosebud is a well-liked AI journal. Its [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/rosebud-ai-journal-diary/id6451135127) had a 4.9 rating from 3.4K ratings when we checked, and it offers guided journals, voice journaling, AI personas, long-term memory and a weekly insight report on iPhone, Android and the web. People still go looking for apps like Rosebud, usually for one of these reasons.

1. **The free plan ended.** Rosebud's help center says that "effective September 30, 2026, the Rosebud free plan will be retired," because it can't sustainably offer a free tier with its new memory system ([source](https://help.rosebud.app/account/changes-to-the-free-plan)). Its homepage still said "Rosebud is free to use" on November 19, 2026, but the help center page is the specific one.
2. **The price.** Rosebud Bloom costs $12.99 a month or $107.99 a year on its [homepage](https://www.rosebud.app/), and its App Store listing adds Thrive tiers up to $59.99 a month. [Usage limits](https://help.rosebud.app/ai-analysis/usage-limits) reset weekly, and Rosebud doesn't publish the numbers.
3. **Memory you can't see.** Rosebud's [long-term memory](https://help.rosebud.app/ai-analysis/long-term-memory) is on for Bloom subscribers, and Rosebud says it "does better recalling topics vs. specific dates." It doesn't state whether you can view, edit or delete what it remembers.
4. **No meditation.** We found no meditation or breathing feature on Rosebud's site, listing or help center, so people who want both often run two apps.
5. **Reliability reports.** A few App Store and Trustpilot reviewers mention lost voice recordings or replies losing context as their journal grew. Those are individual user reports, not something we could measure.

If you leave, Rosebud says you can [export everything](https://help.rosebud.app/account/export-data) as Markdown from the web app, and nothing you wrote is deleted when the free plan ends.

## The best Rosebud alternatives, app by app

### 1. Auserene: best Rosebud alternative for meditation and readable memory

Read this section knowing we wrote it. Auserene is an AI journal for iPhone built around one evening conversation. During the day you leave quick notes: a line, a photo, a voice note transcribed in over 100 languages, a page of handwriting scanned on the phone, or a mood on a slider and an 82-word Feelings Wheel. In the evening, Auserene reads those notes, asks about the parts that seem to matter, and writes the journal entry from the conversation. Then it writes a guided meditation from that day, five, 15 or 25 minutes long, with your name and your actual worries in it.

What it remembers lives in the You tab as plain sentences you can edit or delete: what seems to help you, what matters, what gets to you, threads still open. Moods show up month by month, a week in review looks back on the last seven days, each person in your life has a page with shared moments and relationship insights, and habit goals have a 21, 42 or 64-day finish line with no streaks. There are 13 guided conversations, eight guided meditation journeys and 11 ambient sounds.

The free plan covers notes with photos and scanning, mood logging, an evening walk through your notes on your own, three "Help me write" nudges a day, free journeys and sounds, habit goals, people and widgets. Premium, at $11.99 a month or $105.99 a year with seven days free, adds the conversations, memory, voice notes and the meditation from your day. Our [privacy policy](/privacy-policy) covers encryption with a per-user key, AI providers under zero-data-retention contracts that bar training, and no ads.

Skip it if you use Android or journal on a laptop, because Auserene is iPhone only. Rosebud's CBT, ACT and IFS guided journals also have no direct match here. For a closer head-to-head, see [Auserene vs Rosebud](/blog/auserene-vs-rosebud).

### 2. Mindsera: best for analysis and mental models

Mindsera leans toward thinking more than talking. Its [homepage](https://mindsera.com/) describes conversational prompts from "a companion who knows about your past," Ask Your Journal, entry analysis, long-term emotions analysis, a voice mode and Call Mode, more than 52 thinking frameworks, a habit tracker, and connections to Oura and Whoop. Its [plan comparison](https://mindsera.com/help/free-plan-vs-paid-plan) says the free Curious plan includes journaling, habits, frameworks and export, with one-time samples of the AI features; AI memory and ongoing AI need Genius, which its help page prices at $10.75 to $14.99 a month.

Best for people who liked Rosebud's insights and want more structured analysis.

### 3. Life Note: best for journaling with AI "mentors"

Life Note's pitch is journaling "with history's greatest minds." Its [homepage](https://www.mylifenote.ai/) lists more than 1,000 AI mentors that remember your past entries, Connect the Dots, Talk to Your Inner Wisdom, a Weekly Letter, an Inner Portrait, an Inner Weather emotion map, voice journaling, and connections that let ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini read your journal. Best for people who want a Rosebud alternative with a free tier and a playful, many-voices format.

Best for people who want Rosebud's mix of guided journals and AI reviews at a lower price, on every device.

### 5. Day One: best for a long-term archive with AI added

Day One is a journal first. Its [pricing page](https://dayoneapp.com/pricing/) lists a free Basic plan with unlimited text entries, Silver at $49.99 a year and Gold at $74.99 a year, with the AI features in Gold: Daily Chat, Go Deeper prompts, entry summaries, title suggestions and image generation. [Daily Chat](https://dayoneapp.com/guides/ai-features/daily-chat/) asks about your day, turns the conversation into an entry and builds memories of your routines and relationships. Day One's guide notes that reviewing or deleting individual memories isn't available in its iPhone, iPad or Mac apps.

Day One offers end-to-end encryption on every plan, and says plainly that Daily Chat content goes to the AI provider outside that encryption. It says that content isn't used to train AI models. It runs on nearly everything: iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Watch, Android and the web.

Best for people who want years of entries, photos and video in one place, with a Rosebud-style chat as an optional extra.

Best for people who want morning and evening check-ins with some generic meditation in the same app.

### 7. Apple Journal: best free and private option

Apple's [Journal app](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/journal/id6447391597) is free on iPhone, iPad and Mac, and syncs between them through iCloud. It suggests moments to write about using on-device intelligence, transcribes voice entries, shows insights and streaks, and can log your state of mind to the Health app. Apple says entries are [end-to-end encrypted in iCloud](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/data/en/journaling-suggestions/) and suggestions are processed on the device.

There's no AI chat and no memory of what you've written. Best for people who leave Rosebud mainly over price or privacy and don't need the AI to talk back.

### 8. ChatGPT: best if you want to set it up yourself

Plenty of people journal in ChatGPT. It can hold a conversation about your day, and its memory can carry details forward. You can view and delete saved memories, but deleting a chat doesn't remove a memory made from it, and OpenAI may train on personal accounts' chats unless you turn that off in Data controls. Temporary Chat isn't used for training and doesn't create memories. Our post on [using ChatGPT as a journal](/blog/chatgpt-as-a-journal) covers the settings to change first and why a chatbot that agrees with everything can make rumination worse.

There's no mood tracking, weekly report or meditation built in. Best for people who already pay for ChatGPT and are comfortable configuring it.

### 9. Reflectly: best for a light daily mood check-in

Reflectly calls itself "the world's first intelligent journal." Its [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/reflectly-journal-ai-diary/id1241229134) had 4.6 stars from 82K ratings and describes AI-guided prompts and a mood tracker with correlations and graphs, with premium in-app purchases from $4.99 to $59.99. It doesn't state its AI provider, encryption or training policy.

Best for people who want a quick daily check-in more than a conversation.

## How to choose

1. **Decide what you want the AI to do.** Ask questions and write with you (Auserene, Day One's Daily Chat, ChatGPT), analyze you (Mindsera, Reflection), or speak in many voices (Life Note).
2. **Check whether you can read the memory.** An app that remembers you should show you what it remembers and let you fix it. Auserene, Mindsera (its [Memory Profile](https://mindsera.com/help/account/memory-profile)) and ChatGPT let you see and edit what they remember; Day One doesn't in its Apple apps; the others don't state it.
3. **Find the training default.** Most journal apps here say your entries don't train AI. ChatGPT does by default unless you opt out.
4. **Know the encryption you're getting.** Apple Journal and Day One offer end-to-end encryption, and Day One lifts it for AI. Every app that sends your words to a cloud AI model has to read them to reply.
5. **Check the free plan's terms, today.** Rosebud's ended this year. Read what a plan includes now and whether you can export.
6. **Match the platform to your life.** If you write on a laptop or an Android phone, an iPhone-only app won't fit.
7. **Try a week of evenings.** Notice whether you close the app calmer or more wound up. That tells you more than any table, this one included.

None of these apps is therapy, ours included. ## Questions people ask about Rosebud alternatives

### What is the best alternative to Rosebud?

It depends on what you liked about Rosebud. For an AI conversation plus a meditation made from your day and memory you can edit, Auserene (iPhone only). For analysis and frameworks, Mindsera. For a cheaper all-device option with guided journals, Reflection. For a long archive with optional AI, Day One. For free and private with no AI chat, Apple Journal.

### Is there a free alternative to Rosebud?

Yes. Reflection, Life Note and Mindsera have free plans with limited AI, Day One's free plan has no AI, Apple Journal is free with no AI chat, and Auserene's free plan covers notes, mood, habits, people and guided journeys, with the AI conversation on Premium.

### Why did Rosebud get rid of its free plan?

Rosebud's help center says it retired the free plan on September 30, 2026, because it is "unable to sustainably offer a free tier moving forward," tied to the cost of its new memory system. Existing entries stay and can be exported.

### What apps are like Rosebud journal?

Apps like Rosebud include Auserene, Mindsera, Life Note, Reflection, Day One (with its Gold plan's Daily Chat), Stoic and Reflectly. All of them use AI to respond to or analyze your journal; they differ on memory, meditation, platforms and price.

### Is there a Rosebud alternative with meditation?

Auserene writes a guided meditation from each day's conversation and has eight guided meditation journeys. Stoic includes generic meditation and breathing exercises. Rosebud, Mindsera, Life Note, Reflection and Day One don't list meditation.

### Can I move my Rosebud journal to another app?

Rosebud lets you export all entries as Markdown from its web app, and individual entries on web and mobile. Whether another app can import them depends on that app.
