## Why people look for a Calm or Headspace alternative

Calm and Headspace are the two biggest names in meditation for good reason. Calm's [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/calm/id571800810) had a 4.8 rating from 2M ratings when we checked, and Headspace's [listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/headspace-sleep-meditation/id493145008) had 4.8 from 974K. Both have huge libraries, well-known teachers, sleep content, and apps on almost every device. People still go looking for something else, usually for one of these reasons.

1. **The library doesn't know your day.** A ten-minute session on stress was recorded for everyone. On the night your manager moved a deadline and your sister didn't text back, it doesn't mention either.
2. **Price.** Both charge yearly subscriptions, and both run promotions that make the first year look different from the second.
3. **Too much choice.** A library of hundreds of sessions can leave you scrolling instead of sitting.
4. **You journal somewhere else.** Many people write in one app and meditate in another, and the two never meet.

Our research roundup on [personalized meditation](/blog/personalized-meditation) looks at what the studies say. In short, tailoring health content helps a little on average, the evidence specific to digital mental health is thin, and we couldn't find a published trial comparing AI-written meditations with standard recordings. Where personalization looks most promising is in getting people to come back, not in making each session stronger. Keep that in mind when any app, including ours, promises a lot.

## Calm vs Headspace: what each one does best

If you haven't settled on either, here's the honest version.

**Calm** is strongest at sleep. Its [homepage](https://www.calm.com/) says it has more than 500 Sleep Stories, with narrators including Matthew McConaughey, Harry Styles and Jennifer Garner, plus soundscapes, sleep music and white, brown and green noise. For daytime practice it has the 7 Days of Calm and 21 Days of Calm programs, daily sessions with Tamara Levitt, Jay Shetty and Jeff Warren, a 60-second Breathe Bubble, Panic SOS, and a Sleep Check-In that shows patterns in how you slept. Calm runs on iOS, Android and the web, plus Apple TV, Apple Watch, Wear OS and Samsung Health. Its [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/calm/id571800810) says there are never any ads and that some programs and features are "free forever."

**Headspace** is strongest at teaching the skill and at support beyond meditation. Its [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/headspace-sleep-meditation/id493145008) describes more than 500 guided meditations, sleepcasts, focus music, mindful movement and progress tracking, and its [homepage](https://www.headspace.com/) says a subscription includes "1,000+ expert-led exercises." Headspace also offers mental health coaching, therapy through some plans, and [Ebb](https://www.headspace.com/ebb), an AI companion covered below. It runs on iPhone, iPad and Android.

On price, Headspace is the easier one to pin down. Its App Store listing says $12.99 a month or $69.99 a year in the US, and its [subscriptions page](https://www.headspace.com/subscriptions) offers a 14-day free trial. When we checked, the homepage was running 50% off the first year, after which the plan "automatically renews at full price." Calm's website asked us to create an account before showing a price, and its App Store listing shows Calm Premium purchases at $14.99, $16.99, $69.99 and $79.99 without saying which is monthly or yearly.

One inconsistency is worth knowing about. Calm's homepage says "over 500 Sleep Stories," while its App Store listing says "100+ exclusive Sleep Stories." The second figure may count only exclusives, but Calm doesn't say.

If sleep is the problem, start with Calm. If you want to learn to meditate, or want coaching in the same app, start with Headspace.

## What makes a meditation app personalized?

Most apps say they personalize. It helps to ask which kind. Our [personalized meditation](/blog/personalized-meditation) piece sorts it into four levels.

1. **You pick from a library.** Sleep or focus, five or 20 minutes. This is what most apps mean.
2. **A questionnaire picks for you.** You answer a few questions and rules route you to a track.
3. **The session adapts to signals.** Pacing, length or sound changes based on what you report or what a sensor reads.
4. **The words are written for you.** The script itself comes from what you've said, so it can mention what actually happened today.

Calm and Headspace mostly work at the first level, with Headspace's Ebb adding a conversation that leads to a recommendation. Balance works at the second and third. Endel works at the third, for sound rather than guidance. Auserene works at the fourth. The fourth is the newest and has the least direct evidence, which is why we'd rather you try it and judge for yourself than take our word for it.

## Is there a meditation app that knows you?

Among the apps above, here is what each one can actually know.

1. **Calm** knows how you slept, if you use its Sleep Check-In.
2. **Headspace** knows, through Ebb, what you've told it in chat.
3. **Balance** knows your answers to its daily questions.
4. **Endel** knows your location, your environment and your heart rate.
5. **Auserene** knows what you wrote and said today, and what it has learned over time, which you can read and edit.

None of Calm, Headspace, Balance, Waking Up, Insight Timer, Stoic or Endel says it writes a meditation from your journal or your day. That's the gap Auserene was built for. Whether a meditation that knows your day is better for you is something only a week of trying can tell you. ## How to choose a Calm or Headspace alternative

1. **Name the problem.** Sleep, stress, learning to meditate, or feeling like the app doesn't get you. Each points to a different app.
2. **Decide how much personalization you want.** A library you choose from (Insight Timer, Waking Up), sessions picked or assembled for you (Balance, Headspace's Ebb), or sessions written from your day (Auserene).
3. **Check the device.** Calm and Headspace run on Android, and Calm on the web. Auserene is iPhone only.
4. **Look past the first-year price.** Promotions are common, and renewal is usually at full price.
5. **Try one for a week, not one session.** The question is whether you're still pressing play on day seven.

## Questions people ask about Calm and Headspace alternatives

### What is the best alternative to Calm and Headspace?

It depends on why you're looking. For personalization, Balance assembles a session from your answers each day and Auserene writes a meditation from your day. For a free option, Insight Timer. For depth, Waking Up. For sleep, Calm itself is hard to beat.

### Is Calm or Headspace better?

Calm is stronger for sleep, with more than 500 Sleep Stories on its website and the widest device support. Headspace is stronger for learning to meditate and adds coaching, therapy through some plans and Ebb, its AI companion. Headspace costs $12.99 a month or $69.99 a year in the US as of November 2026.

### Is there a free alternative to Calm or Headspace?

Insight Timer offers thousands of guided meditations for free, with an optional Premium plan for courses and offline listening. Calm says some of its programs are free forever. Auserene's free plan includes its free guided journeys and ambient sounds, while the meditation written from your day is Premium.

### Is there an AI meditation app that personalizes sessions?

Yes, in different ways. Headspace's Ebb is an AI companion that recommends Headspace content. Endel generates soundscapes from your location and heart rate. Balance assembles sessions from your answers. Auserene uses AI to write a guided meditation from the conversation you had about your day.

### Does Headspace have an AI companion?

Yes. Headspace's Ebb is an AI companion you can talk to by voice or text. It recommends meditations and activities and remembers past conversations. Headspace says it is available to paid members aged 18 and up in the US, UK, Canada and Australia, in English.

### What is a good meditation app with journaling?

Stoic combines a full journal with generic guided and unguided meditation. Auserene is a journal that writes a guided meditation from each day's entry. Neither Calm nor Headspace lists a written journal.

None of these apps is therapy. If anxiety, low mood or sleeplessness is getting in the way of your life, please talk to a doctor or a mental health professional, and if you're in danger now, our [crisis resources page](/crisis-resources) lists free helplines by country.
