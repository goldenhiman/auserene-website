## Privacy: Apple Journal vs Auserene

Apple Journal is the more private of the two, and we'd rather say that plainly.

Apple says that "when your device is locked with a passcode, entries in the Journal app are encrypted," and that with default two-factor authentication and a passcode, "all Journal entries are end-to-end encrypted when stored in iCloud, so even Apple can't read them" ([source](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/data/en/journaling-suggestions/)). Apple's [iCloud data security overview](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102651) lists Journal data as end-to-end encrypted under standard data protection. One catch is that entries "aren't included in iCloud backups," so Apple points you to an encrypted local backup of your iPhone ([source](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/protect-your-journal-entries-iph9c59b1557/ios)).

Auserene's [privacy policy](/privacy-policy) says your journal is encrypted with AES-256 at rest, using a key unique to you, and travels over TLS. Our team doesn't read your journal, unless you choose to share a conversation with us in a feedback report. The AI that reads your journal works under zero-data-retention terms, keeps nothing after a reply and is contractually barred from training on your words. No ads, no ad trackers, photos of people stay on your phone, a Face ID lock, and you can delete everything from inside the app.

Auserene is not end-to-end encrypted. To talk with you about your day, our servers and the AI provider have to process your words in that moment. That's the trade for a journal that answers.
