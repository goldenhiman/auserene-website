Auserene vs Apple Journal comes down to whether you want your journal to talk back. Apple Journal is free, end-to-end encrypted in iCloud, and runs on iPhone, iPad and Mac. It suggests moments to write about using on-device processing, and on iOS 27 it can suggest writing prompts, but it doesn't hold a conversation or remember what you've told it. Auserene is an iPhone-only AI journal that reads the notes you left during the day, talks with you about them in the evening, writes the entry, remembers the people and threads in your life, and can turn the day into a guided meditation. That costs money for the AI parts, and it is not end-to-end encrypted.

## What is Apple Journal?

Journal is Apple's own journaling app. Its [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/journal/id6447391597) describes it as a way to "take a moment to reflect on your day." It's free, needs iOS 17.2 or later, sits in the Health & Fitness category, and had 4.8 stars from 345K ratings when we checked.

The listing covers suggested moments (outings, photos, workouts, media) and reflection prompts about things like gratitude and purpose, entries with inline photos, videos, places, drawings and voice recordings, multiple journals, search, a calendar, a Places map, PDF export, streaks, reminders, widgets, and state of mind logging to Health.

It's no longer iPhone and iPad only. Apple publishes a [Journal user guide for Mac](https://support.apple.com/guide/journal/welcome/mac) covering macOS Tahoe 26 and macOS 27, and its iPhone guide says your entries "appear on your iPhone, iPad, and Mac where you're signed in to the same Apple Account" ([source](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/protect-your-journal-entries-iph9c59b1557/ios)).

## What is Auserene?

Auserene is an AI journal for iPhone. During the day you leave quick notes: a line of text, a photo, a voice note, a handwritten or printed page scanned into text on the phone, or a mood. In the evening, Auserene reads the day's notes and holds a short conversation, asking about the parts that seem to matter. That conversation becomes the day's journal entry. Then it can write a guided meditation from the conversation and read it aloud, with your name said the way you say it.

Around that loop there's a chat you can open any time that already knows the people and context in your life, 13 guided conversations (untangle a thought, loosen a worry, think a decision through, put something down), a page for each person who matters, habit goals, and a You tab that lists what Auserene has learned in plain sentences you can edit or delete. It's not therapy and doesn't diagnose.

## How do they journal differently?

Apple Journal is a blank page with good starting points. You open it, maybe tap a suggested moment (a walk, a dinner with photos, a song you played on repeat), and write. Apple's [guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/write-in-your-journal-iph9824e83ce/ios) describes Journaling Suggestions that "intelligently group outings, photos, workouts, and more to help you remember and reflect on your experiences." The writing is up to you.

Auserene splits the work across the day. Notes are meant to be fast, a sentence while you're waiting for the kettle. The reflecting happens in the evening conversation, where the questions come from what you wrote earlier. If your note said "rough call with Mum," the conversation might ask what made it rough, instead of you facing an empty page at 10 p.m.

Neither approach is better for everyone. If you like writing, and the page itself helps you think, Apple Journal gets out of your way. If you mostly don't journal because you don't know what to write, a conversation can carry you further. Our post on [evening reflection questions](/blog/evening-reflection-questions) has questions that work in either app.

## Does Apple Journal have AI?

It has two smart features, and neither is a conversation.

**Journaling Suggestions** use on-device processing. Apple's [Journaling Suggestions privacy page](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/data/en/journaling-suggestions/) says they can draw on your workouts, music and podcasts, the people you texted or called, photos, significant locations and state of mind logs, and that you choose which categories are used and which suggestions go into an entry. Apple's [settings guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/change-journal-settings-iphf965002cf/ios) says the data used is "private and stored on your iPhone."

**Writing Prompts** arrived with Apple Intelligence. Apple's iOS 27 guide says Journal "can suggest customized writing prompts based on what you write and any photos or drawings you add to your entry," up to three per entry ([source](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/write-in-your-journal-iph9824e83ce/ios)). They need an iPhone 15 Pro or later, a supported language and an adult account, and Apple notes usage limits may apply. Apple's Journal pages don't state whether the prompts are made on the device or in the cloud.

That's where it stops. We didn't find a chat, a summary of your week, or any feature that remembers what you wrote last month in Apple's Journal guide.

## Which one remembers you?

This is the biggest difference between the two.

Apple Journal keeps your entries, lets you search them, and shows your streak and days journaled in Insights ([guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/build-a-journaling-habit-iph70107aec2/ios)). It doesn't build a picture of you that it uses later. For some people that's exactly the point.

Auserene does build that picture, and shows it to you. On Premium, it remembers across everything you've told it: who your sister is, what tends to help on a bad day, what keeps getting to you, threads that are still open. All of that sits on the You tab in plain sentences. If something's wrong or no longer true, you edit or delete it, and the next conversation reflects the change. It also writes relationship insights on each person's page from what you've said about them, and a week in review that looks back on the last seven days.

## Mood tracking: state of mind vs the Feelings Wheel

Both apps borrow from the same idea, that naming a feeling precisely is useful.

In Apple Journal you tap a button while writing, drag a slider for how you feel, tap words that describe it, then tap what's having the biggest impact. That state of mind goes to the Health app, "where you can review its history over time." You can also log journaling time as Mindful Minutes ([guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/journal-for-your-wellbeing-iph7b79617d5/ios)).

Auserene starts with a quick slider, then offers a Feelings Wheel with 82 words for what you feel and what caused it, and shows moods month by month. Our post on [naming your feelings](/blog/name-your-feelings) covers why the extra words help.

## Does Apple Journal have meditation?

No. Apple Journal has no meditation, breathing or guided audio. The Mindful Minutes it records are your journaling time.

Auserene writes a guided meditation from your evening conversation, five, 15 or 25 minutes long, using what you actually talked about, and reads it aloud with your name said the way you say it. There are also eight guided journeys of a few minutes a day (sleep, an anxious mind, focus, pressure, self-kindness, noticing the good and more), 11 ambient sounds that mix under the voice, and breathing sessions that keep running on the Lock Screen. Audio plays offline once you've heard it. The free journeys and sounds are on the free plan; the meditation from your day is Premium. Auserene's voice notes run up to 10 minutes and are transcribed in over 100 languages, and replies in chat can be read aloud. Voice notes are a Premium feature.

## Privacy: Apple Journal vs Auserene

Apple Journal is the more private of the two, and we'd rather say that plainly.

Apple says that "when your device is locked with a passcode, entries in the Journal app are encrypted," and that with default two-factor authentication and a passcode, "all Journal entries are end-to-end encrypted when stored in iCloud, so even Apple can't read them" ([source](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/data/en/journaling-suggestions/)). Apple's [iCloud data security overview](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102651) lists Journal data as end-to-end encrypted under standard data protection. You can lock Journal with Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode. One thing to know is that Journal entries "aren't included in iCloud backups." They sync through iCloud, and for a separate backup Apple points you to an encrypted local backup of your iPhone ([source](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/protect-your-journal-entries-iph9c59b1557/ios)).

Auserene encrypts your writing with a key tied to your account, and no one on our team reads it. The AI providers we use work under zero-data-retention contracts, keep nothing after a reply and are contractually barred from training on what you write. There are no ads or ad trackers, photos of people stay on the phone, and you can delete your account and everything in it from inside the app. But Auserene is not end-to-end encrypted. To talk with you about your day, our servers and the AI provider have to process your text in memory. That's the trade for an AI that answers. Our [privacy policy](/privacy-policy) has the details.

## Can you use Apple Journal and Auserene together?

Yes, and some people will. Apple Journal is good at what Auserene doesn't try to do: long-form writing on a Mac, photo-heavy entries tied to places, and PDF exports. Auserene is for the evening conversation, the memory and the meditation. If you already keep years of entries in Apple Journal, there's no reason to abandon them.

## Which should you choose?

Choose Apple Journal if:

1. You want a journal that's free and end-to-end encrypted.
2. You like writing and don't want an AI reading or answering it.
3. You write on an iPad or a Mac as well as your iPhone.
4. You want entries you can print and export as PDFs.

Choose Auserene if:

1. You want the journal to ask you questions and write the entry from your answers.
2. You want it to remember the people and threads in your life, and to see and edit what it remembers.
3. You want a meditation made from your own day, not a separate meditation app.
4. You journal by voice in a language other than English.
5. You're comfortable with strong encryption that isn't end-to-end, in exchange for an AI that talks back.

It depends on what you want. Apple Journal is better on price (free), privacy (end-to-end encrypted in iCloud) and devices (iPhone, iPad and Mac).
